The idea

Sensors tell us what is happening.
Language connects it to what comes next.

Sensor models typically recognize states or predict outcomes. Sensor-Language-Action (SLA) modeling brings actions into the same framework: using language to connect heterogeneous sensor histories, individual context, and decision-relevant evidence.

The model

One model for actions and their evidence.

OpenSLA combines encoded sensor histories and language context in a shared language backbone, with structured action heads and an action-conditioned multi-modal fusion decoder.

OpenSLA-B

Direct sensor-language modeling

Encoded waveform and numerical tokens join language context in the backbone. Hierarchical action heads predict actions; the fusion decoder conditions evidence generation on those predictions.

OpenSLA-H

Hierarchical Memory

Local, temporal, and global queries summarize long sensor histories. Detail selectors retrieve pre-compression sensor features for the fusion decoder.

Results & insights

From physiological signals to meaningful language.

We evaluate OpenSLA on action prediction, sensor-state understanding, and action explanation across three domains, and examine what its learned representations capture.

Fig. 4

Sensor-grounded caption generation

OpenSLA describes both physiological states and fine-grained signal statistics. In this clinical example, its caption captures heart-rate and ECG findings that the LLM baseline misses.

Fig. 6

Action structure and future-state information

Interpolating between learned representations retrieves a continuous progression of insulin actions. Separately, trained readouts recover future glucose summaries from frozen representations, revealing information beyond the action targets used in backbone training.

Transfer to an external cohort

Clinical models are evaluated on waveform-linked MIMIC-IV without cohort-specific adaptation, using the same Clinical action vocabulary.

Generalization to an unseen label

A focused MIMIC-III study tests Imipenem, an unseen fine-grained label within a known antibiotic category, against no-action windows.
